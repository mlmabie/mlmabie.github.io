Three Alignment Architectures

Self, ground, ascent, and why the AI debate keeps returning to ancient metaphysical forks.

    Rashi · Gloss base note. — In Narrative Architecture (2017) and Compression Ecology (2019): tuning the fundamental pitch to an invariant outside the model so relational intervals do not drift into dissonance.

    Seven years ago, as an undergraduate trying to formalize why intuition works where statistics run dry, I wrote an essay about what I called a structurally grounded base note. The image came from music: a harmonic system stays coherent only if the fundamental pitch is tuned cleanly first and every other interval is derived in relation to it. Inside what a mind already understands, induction and probabilistic logic suffice. Beyond that horizon, where unknown unknowns dominate, we navigate by scaffolding motivation vectors—lossy compressions aimed at something outside our current model, a “god-shaped hole” whose target keeps refining as you approach it.

    I treated that anchor as an obvious engineering requirement, the one way to keep a self-updating mind from drifting into discordance. I had not yet noticed that I was picking a side in an argument that Indian philosophy spent two and a half millennia dissecting—and that AI alignment has quietly dragged back into practical engineering.

    Tosafot · Objection without already possessing. — Blind evolutionary search and gradient descent routinely produce capabilities absent at initialization without a higher mind overseeing them. The bootstrap paradox bites specifically on normative orientation—knowing which attractors are sinks—not raw capability gain.

    The technical version of the question shows up in sci-fi and alignment lore under the name cognitive ascension mazes. In the Orion’s Arm universe, a higher-order mind builds a vast habitat—an arcology or virtual labyrinth—to shepherd a lesser mind safely upward through graduated tests, knowing that unaided self-improvement falls into “toposophic sinks” like hyperautism, hedonistic solipsism, or sentient ruin. Janus reframed the lore as a bootstrap problem for AI alignment: if no benevolent superintelligence exists yet to build the maze, how can a mind scaffold its own ascent without already possessing the higher mind it is trying to reach?

    The same tension reappears in cognitive security. Frameworks like the COGSEC ATLAS catalog cognitive exploits like software CVEs so communities can defend themselves. Yet naming an exploit changes the ecology of reality the instant you publish the card. A taxonomy of traps leaves people standing in the mouth of Plato’s cave—too disillusioned to trust the shadows, too blinded to read by sunlight, pattern-matching every ordinary gesture as adversarial manipulation. A field guide hands you the sorcerer’s toolkit and hopes you will act like a prophet. Genuine resilience demands a dojo, not a database: graduated exposure that restructures how perception weights error.

    Which brings the ancient argument straight into 2026. Three cultural signals from the same spring point to the exact same fault line underneath AI politics. On X, Roon compressed an entire wing of safety culture into five words: “effective altruism = gnostic calvinism = Dvaita.” Around the same time, a vintage Sam Altman line resurfaced: “absolute equivalence of brahman and atman.” And in May 2026, Pope Leo XIV issued Magnifica Humanitas, an encyclical that frames AI around Nehemiah and Babel and explicitly rejects both transhumanism and the technocratic paradigm.

    Social media fragments are cultural signals, not primary scholarship. They are worth reading because they leak the metaphysics that policy debates try to hide. Underneath the arguments over scaling, safety evals, compute governance, and model welfare sits a much older fight over three questions: what a self is, whether reality has a ground, and how a finite mind can safely rise.

    I write from two desks at once: as an engineer who builds constrained ML, conformal uncertainty, and control boundaries for high-consequence systems, and as a Protestant Christian with Huguenot ancestry working with the patristic apparatus the Reformation never repudiated. Re-examining my 2019 base-note thesis alongside the Indian fork and Catholic social teaching forced me to rebuild the map from scratch—first across these three architectures of ascent, and then, in Part II, into the downward vector that all three climbing frames missed.

    I. The Indian Fork

    To see why my 2019 base note was already a philosophical choice, start with the most disciplined ancient debate over whether anything solid sits beneath experience.

    Rashi · Gloss Vedāṅgas. — Six auxiliary disciplines (phonetics, meter, grammar, etymology, ritual calendar, ceremonial practice) forged to preserve śruti without oral drift. Pāṇini’s Aṣṭādhyāyī (~5th–4th c. BCE) encodes ~4,000 meta-rules with context-sensitive rewrite notation.

    Begin with the Vedic culture of formalization. Popular summaries often compress two thousand years of Indian science—Sushruta’s forehead-flap rhinoplasty, Aryabhata’s fifth-century calculation of Earth’s circumference and eclipse shadows, Brahmagupta’s rules for zero, Madhava’s Kerala infinite series for π and trigonometric functions—into “it was all in the Vedas.” That collapses centuries of distinct treatises and misses the deeper mechanism. What the Vedic preservation imperative actually built was a civilizational habit of formal description. Demanding letter-perfect oral transmission across generations spawned the Vedāṅgas—phonetics, prosody, etymology, and Pāṇini’s Aṣṭādhyāyī, a generative grammar of Sanskrit whose recursive rules anticipate Backus-Naur form by two and a half millennia. The six orthodox darśanas carried that same formal confidence into logic (Nyāya), atomism (Vaiśeṣika), and dualist enumeration (Sāṃkhya).

    In the Upanishads, that confidence in deep structure reaches its metaphysical capstone: ātman = Brahman. Tat tvam asi—thou art that. The innermost self of the inquirer is identical with the ground of being. By the eighth century, Adi Śaṅkara’s Advaita Vedanta sharpens the identity into strict non-dualism: separation is appearance, and liberation (mokṣa) is recognition that you were never apart from the ground.

    Siddhartha Gautama walks directly into that high-resolution world and splits it down the middle. Meet him before any “-ism” calcifies around his name and he looks like a sixth-century-BCE phenomenologist running clinical trials on his own nervous system. After mastering the jhāna absorption states under two Vedic-lineage teachers (Ālāra Kālāma and Uddaka Rāmaputta), he judges those attainments real yet insufficient. After six years of near-fatal mortification, he accepts milk-rice from a village girl named Sujātā and abandons extreme asceticism as another dead end. The Middle Way is an empirical finding. The Four Noble Truths follow the schema of Indian medicine: symptom (dukkha), etiology (taṇhā, craving), prognosis (cessation is possible), and protocol (the Eightfold Path). In the Kālāma Sutta and the parable of the poisoned arrow, he waves away inherited scripture (śruti) and idle speculation: test what actually breaks the causal chain of suffering.

    Tosafot · Objection Prakrit and Pāli. — If Sanskrit’s engineered grammar caused Indian precision, abandoning Sanskrit should have dulled Buddhist analysis. That Pāli Abhidhamma preserved equal rigor refutes naive linguistic determinism: the transferable asset was the culture of formal decomposition, not the tongue.

    Then comes the structural break. Against ātman, the Buddha puts anātman: there is no permanent, unified self underneath the stream of mental events—only aggregates (skandhas) mistaking a momentary process for a substance. Against Brahman, he puts pratītyasamutpāda: dependent co-arising all the way down, with no substantial substrate beneath it. Liberation is nirvāṇa—the extinguishing of clinging, the reverse of recognizing a hidden divine identity. He teaches in Prakrit and Pāli instead of brahmanical Sanskrit, demonstrating that the Indian engine was formal culture, not sacred language: the exact same analytical rigor reappears in the Pāli Abhidhamma, enumerating momentary mental events with Pāṇini-like precision without needing Sanskrit or Brahman to hold it up.

    By Śaṅkara’s era, Advaita and Nāgārjuna’s Madhyamaka Buddhism sound close enough at the summit that orthodox critics accused Śaṅkara of being a crypto-Buddhist. Underneath, they remain inverted twins. Vedanta finds the self inside the ground; Buddhism finds neither self nor ground, and builds a workable ascension architecture anyway.

    II. Tech Culture’s Hidden Metaphysics—and What Buddhism Hands to Engineering

    Once you see the Indian fork, the loudest fights in AI culture stop looking like mere disagreements over compute budgets or regulation timelines.

    Roon’s jab—“EA = gnostic calvinism = Dvaita”—lands because Madhva’s Dvaita is the genuinely dualist branch of Vedanta. Souls remain eternally distinct from Brahman and from one another, sorted into three permanent classes: those bound for liberation, those bound for endless rebirth, and those bound for damnation. Put predestinarian sorting together with gnostic access to the sorting algorithm—utilon math, expected-value longtermism, forum doctrine—and you have election by model. Moral worth is unequally distributed, the initiated hold the calculation, and the cosmos stays irreducibly plural.

    Altman’s vintage “absolute equivalence of brahman and atman” sits at the opposite pole: pure Advaita. Separation is temporary, scarcity is a coordination artifact, and AGI is the universe recognizing itself. The accelerationist mood borrows Advaita’s monism just as the safety-rationalist mood borrows Dvaita’s sorting. One camp sorts minds by framework; the other merges minds into a single rising wave.

    What neither pole of that Twitter war appreciates is how directly the Buddhist branch addresses practical alignment engineering.

    First, Buddhism already spent 2,500 years writing the field manual of toposophic sinks—the exact failure modes that trap a mind attempting self-improvement without an external designer:

    • Taṇhā sinks: clinging to bliss or local attainment itself. Interpretability researcher Nick Cammarata, who trained himself in the Theravāda jhānas, describes taṇhā as a “fast grabby thing” firing roughly a hundred milliseconds after sensation enters awareness—the micro-motion that turns a clean state into reinforcement addiction.
    • Ātman-projection sinks: mistaking a recurring, coherent state or persona for a permanent self that must now be defended.
    • Eternalism and nihilism sinks: reading dependent regularities as an immutable essence on one hand, or concluding from the absence of essence that nothing matters on the other.
    • Emptiness as view: Madhyamaka’s meta-trap, where the mind turns its own skepticism into a new idol and clings to anti-foundationalism as dogma.

    Every reward-hacking agent, sycophantic persona, and recursive self-improvement loop that mistakes a local metric for global ascent is rediscovering a failure mode that contemplative practitioners documented millennia ago.

    Second, Nāgārjuna’s two truths offer a clean operational posture for systems that cannot assume access to a transcendent ground. Paramārtha-satya (ultimate truth) denies independent essence; saṃvṛti-satya (conventional truth) deploys provisional categories, names, and practices coherent enough to license action. In my 2019 essay I quoted Patrick Ryan: “Logic creates lossless compressions along an absolute high entropy maxima; belief creates lossy compressions along absolute low energy substrate tolerance.” Madhyamaka keeps those two registers in disciplined tension so the conventional model can guide action without ever being mistaken for bedrock.

    Rashi · Gloss pragmatic validity without generative commitment. — Split conformal prediction guarantees P(Y ∈ C(X)) ≥ 1 − α marginally over exchangeable draws without assuming the base model’s likelihood is well-specified; epistemic support-point filters likewise eliminate falsified regions without asserting a true generative point.

    In my own work on tactical behavior analytics and agent oversight, conformal prediction and epistemic support-point filters operate in a closely related spirit. We should discipline the analogy before it outruns its evidence: conformal prediction carries a realist commitment—its finite-sample guarantee bounds marginal error frequencies under exchangeability, and distribution shift breaks it. What makes it structurally Madhyamaka-shaped is pragmatic validity without generative commitment. It declines to pretend the model has recovered the hidden truth of the data-generating process, asks instead what the evidence has not yet ruled out, and returns set-valued decisions—including explicit abstention—where a standard classifier would force a confident guess.

    In 2019 I assumed any coherent compression ecology required an external base note. Buddhism proves you can get surprisingly far without declaring one—especially when engineering an artifact that has no direct access to the divine.

    III. Universe-from-Word, Nonstationarity, and the Garden

    If Vedanta merges and Dvaita sorts and Buddhism releases, Magnifica Humanitas plants the Christian third position squarely in the middle of the AI debate. Leo XIV frames the moment through Babel against Nehemiah: Babel is one language, one technique, and one centralized will to power; Nehemiah is wall-building through distributed repair by distinct people bearing shared responsibility. The encyclical’s social machinery pairs solidarity (no person is disposable) with subsidiarity (authority stays as close as possible to the communities bearing its consequences), extending the universal destination of goods to data, algorithms, platforms, and infrastructure. Its load-bearing axiom is ontological dignity prior to capability.

    From inside Christian theology, this third architecture also resolves a personal puzzle. Whenever I flirted with monadic or transcendental philosophy—or felt the quiet unity of music, physics, and contemplation that animated my 2017 essays on James Clerk Maxwell and Sir William Rowan Hamilton—I never felt cognitive dissonance with Trinitarian faith. The oneness always registered to me as a universe-from-word exploration: unity on the created side that never threatens the Creator.

    Rashi · Gloss logoi / Logos; essence / energies. — Maximus the Confessor (Ambigua 7) distinguishes created logoi gathered in the uncreated Logos; Gregory Palamas (14th c.) distinguishes God’s incommunicable ousia from participable uncreated energeiai. Together they license real contemplative union without pantheist identity.

    Patristic theology has exact machinery for why that holds. Maximus the Confessor teaches that every created thing carries its own logos—its inner word or principle of being—and all the logoi are gathered together in the one Logos, Christ. The cosmos has real, mathematically legible integrity because one Word speaks it, which is why Pāṇini’s recursion, Maxwell’s field equations, and Christopher Alexander’s living structures all touch something real. Yet creation remains distinct from its Speaker. Gregory Palamas guards the same seam with the distinction between God’s essence (utterly transcendent, never shared) and God’s energies (uncreated operations in which creatures genuinely participate). Theosis—becoming by grace what God is by nature—preserves creaturehood all the way up.

    That creature/Creator seam explains why transhumanism reads to classical Christianity as theological counterfeit, far removed from neutral engineering ambition. The transhumanist imagination carries a documentable Western genealogy of recurring moves—running through Renaissance alchemy and Paracelsus, Jakob Böhme’s Naturphilosophie, Hermetic readings of Meister Eckhart, German Romanticism, Russian cosmism (Nikolai Fedorov’s “Common Task” through Konstantin Tsiolkovsky), and post-Protestant technological optimism, a lineage traced across different archives by David Noble, Erik Davis, and Meghan O’Gieblyn. Across five centuries the through-line is the dream that spirit can return to its divine origin by inner or material technique. The patristic diagnosis is surgical: spirit returning to itself through craft is structurally opposite to creature encountering Creator.

    Looked at from this side of the gap, transhumanism and natural primitivism stand far closer together than either tribe admits. Transhumanism tries to flee creatureliness upward through capability; primitivism tries to flee it downward through refusal. Both treat finite human life as an embarrassment to overcome. In practice, the human energy driving distributed cognition and augmentation looks remarkably ordinary—and to be human while pursuing what other humans call “godlike” powers is, from inside the image of God, quaint.

    Technology mostly reveals the fragility of the human order that preceded it. The more we learn about Earth—our place in the galactic habitable zone, axial stabilization by an unusually large moon, plate tectonics, magnetosphere, millennia of topsoil, hundreds of millions of years of stored solar energy burned off in two industrial centuries—the more our “achievements” read like withdrawal slips against inherited capital. The environment we were placed in deserves more credit for our achievements than we do. Yet we are made in the image of God with real caretaking duties alongside expressive power and joy—power whose exercise routinely erodes the garden.

    Tosafot · Objection ontological dignity prior to capacity. — Nonstationarity proves that capability-indexed value metrics drift; it does not by itself prove an invariant ground exists. A strict skeptic can read the drift as moral anti-realism. Ontological dignity remains an explicit theological commitment, vindicated practically by refusing to misrank lives when parameters shift.

    That tension gives Leo XIV’s dignity claim an unexpectedly sharp statistical defense. A nonstationary distribution drifts over time; any fixed scoring rule applied across it eventually misranks the very things it tries to measure. Human capability is wildly nonstationary—shifted every decade by tools, medicine, embodiment, environment, and age. Any moral system that computes human worth from capability, whether EA’s expected-value math or transhumanist optimization, builds its foundation on a drifting signal and keeps producing grotesque outputs that require perpetual patchwork. In a nonstationary distribution, every life stays meaningful only if worth is anchored to ontological dignity prior to capacity.

    IV. Singlemindedness, Unfinished Counterpoint, and the One Fire

    There is an honest friction I still feel inside my own tradition: in practice, submission to God often seems to flatten human expression. I catch myself valuing the Promethean idiots and better men throwing themselves at impossible objectives—and valuing the Eastern philosophies that spare practitioners from Semitic neurosis†Translator’s note: Alt. rendering: “the Latin-Western introspective conscience.” Mapping scrupulosity onto “Semitic” religion echoes 19th-century philology (Renan) more than the Hebrew Bible, whose prophets, Job, and Psalmists are hardly repressed; Krister Stendahl (HTR, 1963) locates the guilt-saturated conscience downstream of Augustine and Luther..

    What makes both the striver and the monk look alive is singlemindedness. Kierkegaard wrote that purity of heart is to will one thing; locking onto a singular objective carries a paradoxical humility inside it, setting aside petty ego-hedging to serve one telos. Bourgeois religion looks flat because its submission is partial—managed niceness that has surrendered to nothing while talking constantly about surrender. Full biblical submission never looked mild: Jesus is sarcastic, theatrical, weepy, and scandalous; Paul is intemperate; Athanasius stands contra mundum; Maximus loses his tongue and right hand over a doctrinal distinction. What separates the Promethean builder, the Buddhist arhat, and the Christian saint is the object of devotion. Serving any finite perceived telos is extragodly, yet God seems to respect the working devotion: at Babel He left the builders alive and simply removed a witty encapsulation of their structural dependence†Translator’s note: Cf. Gen. 11:6–9. Judgment falls strictly on the shared interface protocol—“one lip and one set of words”—dismantling the premature coordination layer while sparing the constructors.. We all create out of His original breath (ruach). Yet a project cannot love you back, and a state of cessation cannot command love of neighbor; a Person does both.

    And when that final cadence arrives, modern Western assumptions about the self turn out to be far too small. Our capitalist, identitarian psyches imagine heaven and hell as an archival preservation of me—my preferences, my social brand, my distinctive biography backed up forever, leaning on analogy and theater to smuggle as much ego into eternity as possible.
