About

The way I work: I go to whatever level the problem actually lives at. Some days that is a configured-axis mismatch between a client and a robot runtime; some days it is what the product should be, how the strategy holds up, or what a team’s culture rewards. The low-level detail keeps the big picture honest, and the big picture decides which details matter, so I move between them as the evidence calls for rather than staying at one altitude.

I’m a research engineer working on robotics and AI systems. At Advanced Metal Research I build run-data, verification, and agent infrastructure across the robotic-welding stack, from weld and materials data through motion research, evaluation, and operator controls, and I build the software-factory and agent workflows the team uses to develop and verify that software.

I began at Applied Medical at sixteen, designing tools for medical-device manufacturing. Over five years, I worked on pneumatic manipulators, fixtures, robotic end-of-arm tooling, prototypes, and production troubleshooting.

Before that, I co-led mechanical design in FIRST Robotics. I also played competitive piano, taught, improvised, and performed.

At UC San Diego I studied Cognitive Science with a specialization in Machine Learning and Neural Computation, alongside reinforcement learning, neural data science, and design research.

At RAM Laboratories, I was principal investigator on a $250k Army SBIR in tactical behavior analytics, a core contributor to RAM’s DARPA AI Cyber Challenge submission, led a joint cognitive-security concept under DARPA FACT, built distributed edge inference, and wrote more than fifteen SBIR proposals. I joined DL-Patcher, automated vulnerability repair, in Phase II and am a named co-inventor on its training method; the patent is pending.

I now work through Mabie Industries. My interests include model evaluation, calibrated decisions, and the tools people use to inspect and correct AI systems.
